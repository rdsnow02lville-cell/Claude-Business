The New Patient Intake Checklist

What actually belongs in your intake packet — and what most practices forget until it costs them.

A good intake packet does three jobs at once: it captures the clinical and billing information your team needs before the visit, it sets expectations with the patient in writing, and it creates the signed documentation that protects the practice later. Most packet problems come from treating it as only the first job.

Patient information

  • Full legal name, preferred name, and date of birth
  • Contact details and preferred contact method — this matters for reminder compliance
  • Emergency contact and relationship
  • Primary language, so you can plan interpretation needs up front

Insurance and billing

  • Primary and secondary carrier, member ID, group number
  • Policyholder details when the patient isn't the policyholder — the most common cause of first-claim denials
  • Assignment of benefits language, so payment flows to the practice

Clinical history

  • Current conditions and diagnoses
  • Medications with dose and frequency — not just names
  • Allergies and reactions; "penicillin" alone doesn't tell a provider whether it was a rash or anaphylaxis
  • Surgical history, primary care physician, referring provider, and preferred pharmacy

The signatures practices forget

This is where packets earn their keep. Beyond the intake form itself, a complete packet gets signatures on:

  1. Consent to treatment — voluntary, revocable, with no guarantee of outcome.
  2. Acknowledgment of your HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices — you're required to make a good-faith effort to obtain this.
  3. Financial policy agreement — copays due at service, self-pay terms, what happens to unpaid balances.
  4. Cancellation and no-show policy — the fee is only enforceable if the patient agreed to it in writing before missing the appointment.

Practical formatting rules

  • Keep the whole packet under six pages; completion rates fall off sharply after that.
  • Use fill-in lines generous enough for real handwriting.
  • Put your practice name, address, and phone on every page — packets get photocopied and separated.
  • Date-version the footer so staff can spot outdated copies in the drawer.

Review it annually

Set a calendar reminder to re-read the packet once a year: fees change, telehealth rules change, and state consent requirements drift. A ten-minute annual review keeps you from discovering a stale form during a dispute.
